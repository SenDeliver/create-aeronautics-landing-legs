Create Aeronautics: Landing Legs — design v1

This is a Create Aeronautics addon for NeoForge 1.21.1. It gives Ships adaptive Legs so they can land on uneven terrain and stay level. The decisions come from the design session on 2026-09-27. This is the original v1 design document, kept for history; the released mod may differ in details (see the README).

mod_id: legworksMC 1.21.1 · NeoForge 21.1.250 · Create 6.0.10 · Aeronautics 1.3.2 · Sable 2.0.5

For terms, see CONTEXT.md (the glossary). For the big decisions and why they were made, see adr/0001 (force legs) and adr/0002 (Mount + Limb).

2.3 Leg Controller

  • Needs no rotation. Placed on the Ship.
  • Linking: click each Leg with the Controller item. Never automatic. At most 8 Legs, within 16 blocks, on the same Ship. A Leg belongs to at most one Controller.
  • Scroll value: Ride Height (lives on the Controller, not the Mount). Default = nominal rest of the Linked Limbs (≈ 2/3 Reach).
  • Redstone signal = Gear Up (the Leg Group folds). No signal = Gear Down.
  • Linking before assembly (assumed): Legs can be linked while the Ship is still a normal build. Links are saved relative to the Controller and checked when the Ship is assembled. Any Leg that ends up on a different Ship is unlinked and shows "Not linked".
  • Simple and Hydraulic Legs can be mixed on one Ship. Simple Legs Level on their own at their fixed default height; the Controller levels only its own Leg Group.
  • Several Controllers per Ship are allowed. All of them aim for level. If their Ride Heights differ, the Ship settles in between and the goggles show "Conflicting Ride Height".
  • Overreach (Ground too uneven): the Legs stretch to the max, the Ship tilts toward the low side, and the goggles show "Overreach".

4. Physics model

Force Legs (ADR 0001)

  • The Leg is one block. It pushes the Ship like a spring through Sable's physics tick, the same hook as propellers.
  • The knee bend is a client-side animation only. Only the Foot touches the world with forces. The drawn leg blocks entities (ADR 0001 amendment, §9.6).
  • Walking later = moving the Foot target, with no jointed bodies.

Ground rules

  • Blocks: their real collision shape (slabs, stairs, snow layers, carpet)
  • Other Ships: the Leg stands on them and pushes them back (a landing pad sinks a little). Confirmed possible: Sable overrides vanilla Level.clip so rays hit other Ships (Offroad wheels use it). Offroad applies no push-back to the other Ship; we add it the way Aeronautics' magnets do (a queued force on the other Ship).
  • Ignored: fluids, entities, non-solid blocks (grass, flowers, torches)

Performance plan

  • Ground check runs once per game tick and caches the Ground hit point and normal. Each physics sub-step recomputes compression and damping from the Mount's current position (reusing a cached distance would make stiff springs unstable).
  • Tuned for Sable at 1 sub-step per tick (sub_level_substeps_per_tick=1; dev default 2). Use the same sable-server.toml setting in the dev run and tune springs under it.
  • A resting, still Ship puts its Legs to sleep (no checks).
  • Knee drawn by a block-entity renderer (like Offroad); the client re-casts its own ray for visuals, so no per-tick sync is needed.
  • Measured with spark before release.
  • Limits live in the server config: 8 Legs per Controller, 16-block link range, Reach 3/4.5/3.5 (proposed, §2.2).

Why: a narrow, tall Ship (7×3, legs ~2.5) is marginal in roll. Soft springs alone rock until they capsize. Three Legs with the centre of mass on the diagonal are neutral, so the fourth Foot must find Ground fast (Crouch), and nothing may unload the opposite Leg.

9.4 Ray rules

  • Pad footprint: 5 rays from the mount face along the leg axis: centre plus ±0.28 on the two perpendicular axes (pad is 10/16 wide). The highest valid support wins, so a pad half over an edge stands on the edge.
  • Centre ray is always support. A side ray more than 0.5 above the Foot's current depth is a Wall. The Foot can't move into it: its velocity into the wall is removed (+0.1 m/s push-out, mass split between Feet). This keeps a Foot in a hole instead of climbing onto the rim. Foot depth = grounded dist, or grows at 3 block/s to Reach after losing Ground. The rule starts after the first contact.
  • Debounce: losing Ground, or a jump deeper than 0.3, is held for 6 ticks.
  • Ground appearing above the Foot (placed block, fast fall, sideways move onto a rim): if the contact came closer than the Ship's own approach (−v·dt) by more than 0.5, rest is cut by the excess. The Foot steps on top in 1 tick with no force spike; real landings still compress.
  • Contact faces: hit accepted when −normal·axis ≥ 0.25 (was 0.5; tilted legs then passed through wall faces).
  • Rays ignore the own Ship (ClipContextExtension.sable$setIgnoredSubLevel). They hit other Ships in their plot coordinates.

9.6 Entity collision on the drawn leg (mixin)

  • A plain large VoxelShape is not enough. Sable's SubLevelEntityCollision.collide only scans blocks overlapping the entity plus one below, and only for Ships whose bounds are near the entity, so a mount 2–3 blocks up is never checked.
  • The spike uses a MixinExtras @WrapOperation in collide. It adds registered leg-mount positions within Reach+1 to the BlockPos.betweenClosed scan, and widens getAllIntersecting by Reach, but only while leg mounts exist.
  • The mount's collision shape = body + 6/16 strut + 10/16 pad at the current Reach, rounded to 1/16. It uses dynamicShape(); server and client each track Reach.
  • Physics is kept out: BlockSubLevelCollisionShape and SableCollisionContext return a full block, and Rapier clamps shapes to one block anyway. Items rested on a 2-block-out leg for 20 s. Players are untested.
  • Pin risk: this targets Sable internals. Pin sable [2.0.5,2.1) and re-test on every update.

  • RCON runner: tools/run_headless.sh <scenario> <log> [-Plegworks.x=y] starts runServer with a fresh world and plays #sleep/command lines through tools/rcon.py. legworks.* Gradle properties become system properties, which is how every tuning variant was A/B tested.
  • Dev commands: /legworks push <at> <impulse>, load <at> <kg> <ticks>, trace 0|1 (per-tick: position, tilt, and per Leg rest/dist/slip or wall/anchor/force/embedded/depth error), mark <name.X>. Also Sable's /sable assemble area, /sable physics translation|impulse.
  • DevScript (client): -Plegworks.script=file with wait/cmd/shot/hud/quit lines. It runs commands on the integrated server and saves screenshots (macOS screencapture is blocked).
  • Tests use one lane per disturbance (a copy of the scene every 30 blocks), so one failure can't spoil the rest.

9.9 Known issues

  • Spinning 20-block drop: a Ship already rotating (about 12°+) at a 20-block impact flips from one-sided leg impulses. Clamping anti-roll and a compression relief valve were tried and made other lanes worse. v2 needs a landing mode (e.g. soft anti-roll for about 0.5 s after touchdown).
  • Narrow, tall Ships are marginal. 40 kg on one corner of an 88 kg 7×3 Ship tips it over. Consider a minimum track width, stiffer Limbs, or a tooltip.
  • Feet catch on ledges (Wall rule): a Ship dragging dangling Feet sideways is stopped by ledges.
  • Deep squat next to an unreachable hole looks odd (legs down to 0.7).
  • Slow position creep under 0.01 block after some pushes.

9.10 Dev setup notes

  • Pin neo_version=21.1.250; the MDK default is 21.1.252 (not .251).
  • The MDK already ships Gradle 9.2.1, which runs on JDK 25; no wrapper bump needed. Java 21 comes via the toolchain plus foojay.
  • Excluding Veil from Sable's POM is fine: Veil loads jar-in-jar in dev on both sides. The Sable POM also pulls companion and sable_rapier separately, which only causes harmless JarSelector warnings.
  • The equal-and-opposite pattern is Simulated's MagnetPair, not Aeronautics'. Offroad raycasts per physics sub-step, not per tick.
